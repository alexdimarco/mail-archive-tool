## Behaviour changes the owner should know

None narrows a guarantee a row states; each follows the design or a finding's own remedy.

- **A lost or damaged extracted attachment is a note, not a failed message** (E6-AO4). The run is Complete, the message is exported with its original and its other files, and README counts it among the messages with an attachment problem. Rulings §13.34 left it open; the design's failure table (derived-file failure: original kept, a note) is the authority.
- **A cancelled message keeps what it extracted and says so** (E6-AO3), and an abandoned run writes nothing more.
- **A zip entry is read through twice**: once to nowhere to check its real size and checksum, then to write (E6-AO2). CPU is about doubled for a zip's entries, bounded by the 4 GiB per-message bound; disk is not touched by a bomb or a damaged entry. The read-through is progress for the no-progress breaker.
- **A zip whose list of entries is over 4 MiB is not opened** (E6-AO7): about 20,000 entries with long names or 90,000 bare ones; the archive writer keeps at most 10,000 parts and a zip of 10,000 entries with 255-byte names still opens. The first-1,000 rule stays for a zip that is opened.
- **Extracted attachments leave room for the later originals** (E6-AI4). The readable copy does not: it stays on its own budget (E5's, pinned by `TestFreeSpacePolicyRefusesAtStart`), so a readable copy can still take space a later original needs on a destination as tight as the plan's estimate; that is E5's, not found here.
- **Attachment names lose controls and invisible marks; a leading dash becomes `_-`** (E6-AO8, E6-AO9), in the export-only pass.

## What held (the finders tried and failed)

- Names that escape `<stem>.attachments/`: `../`, absolute, drive letters, UNC, backslashes, NUL, `~`, `$HOME`, alternate-stream `:stream` and `::$DATA` all end as one segment in the directory (an 81-name corpus and the existing tests).
- Reserved Windows names (AUX, COM1., `Aux .txt`, CONIN$, superscript COM, NUL.tar.gz, LPT1.txt), trailing dots and spaces, 8.3 aliases (they get a hash), names over 255 bytes (cut with a hash, extension kept), and `..`, `...`, `.htaccess`.
- Case and Unicode-fold collisions (Strasse/STRASSE, the ffi ligature, the Kelvin sign, NFC against NFD, trailing-dot variants) become ` (2)`, ` (3)`; the HFS+-ignorable ZWNJ is caught by the no-replace rename. Invalid UTF-8 becomes U+FFFD, so record, manifest and disk agree.
- Planted links: a month directory swapped for a link out of the root and the attachments directory pre-planted as a link both wrote nothing outside the root (`os.Root` refuses; per-part notes). Retries and resumes always make a fresh root, and `ensureDir` is exclusive.
- Zips: a header that lies low stops at the declared size; truthful bombs, per-entry and total bounds on successful entries, the 1,000-entry cut, directory entries, duplicate names, encrypted entries (flag 0x1, AES), unsupported methods and nested zips (written as files, never expanded) behave as designed; a zip changed during the read is irrelevant because it is read back whole under a SHA-256 and size check; no link is ever made from a zip symlink entry.
- Hashes and sizes are taken as written; the temp file is removed on any failure; a panic or error from a part stream is recovered and classified (10,001 mixed faults left no goroutine, no temp and 21 notes); notes carry only `plainText`'d, `%q`-quoted, bounded labels and a 20-note cap with a count note; free-space exhaustion is a per-part note and the R-g stop stays the originals'.
- The `.mailexport-` temp prefix is not special-cased by any sweep, so an attachment named like a temp cannot be deleted by one; a torn run-record tail from a kill during a large append is handled by `truncateTornTail`; two exports use separate roots and ordered record handles; the E6 tests and the finders' probes pass under `-race -count=3`; the app test binary compiles for Windows.

## What this round did not cover

- **A fresh finder's re-verification of the fixes** (process §3). The fixes were re-read by their author after the tests passed, which found one gap (a long read-through of a zip entry was not progress for the no-progress breaker, fixed in `df95d1d`), and the unfiltered suite found another (the readable copy on the new budget, reverted in `bc778fb`), but that is not fresh eyes. It is owed before the option is revealed.
- Windows and macOS runs: the zone-marker effect (E6-AO10), the 260-character effect (E6-AO11), the Windows `Get-FileHash -LiteralPath` check on a long path (E6-AI6a), Windows reserved names on a real volume.
- The production-fsync timings (58 s for 10,001 files, E6-AC1) and the 12-minute and 155 s extrapolations (E6-AC7, E6-AO6) were not re-measured; the fixes remove the quadratic term and bound the rest by the record and the bound, not by a clock.
- Hard links planted in the destination, and a zip changing on disk between the read-back and the extraction, were not attacked by any lens: the first needs same-account access (out of scope), the second cannot happen because both are read back whole under the hash check.
- Free-space exhaustion on a real full disk (only the fakes), and power loss (only the ordering hooks; XC5 says they prove ordering, not durability).
- The README sentences added (no download marker, longer paths, an unbounded folder) are prose a recipient reads; their wording is the fixer's and is not the owner's.
