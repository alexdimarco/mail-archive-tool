**Verdict: HIGH_FRICTION_NOT_SHIPPABLE** — and the bar is named. The happy paths are short and honest, and most refusals say what, why and what to do. What fails is the road back from four ugly situations, and each is a dead end or a pointer to an action that cannot work: **F1** (a stopped archive export cannot be finished once its archive has been copied or moved), **F2** (an archive with hard links or links to other disks previews clean, then exports nothing, with no remedy), **F3** (the card offers "Export failed messages again" when every failure is terminal, and the click is refused) and **F4** (the remedy for another archive folder's index and for records whose files are gone is "Rebuild search index", which cannot act on that folder and, for records whose files are missing, deletes the records). The verdict becomes SHIPPABLE when F1-F4 are built and OW-9, OW-10 and INH-4 are re-walked, and the browser and keyboard click-through of the Source control (OW-20, owner-owned, as for E3) has been done. F5-F20 are the operability backlog that should be built in the same pass; none of them blocks on its own.

**Rationale.** What held up. First export of one folder of the configured archive is 9 clicks with the optional folder-list click, 8 without (open the page, choose the Source, Load folders, Select current folder, type the destination, Preview, Start, Open folder); the first export from another archive folder on a computer that has no Microsoft 365 set up at all is 8, against the design's "about 9" for section 7.4, with Settings and the weekly backup untouched and the inherited folder byte-identical after load, list, preview and run (read-only media included). The preview says what, how much, where and who can read it, and says how many messages have an original `.eml` and how many will be copied as the archived page and zip. An archive drive that stops answering mid-export stops the run cleanly (R-o2: what happened, that nothing in the archive is damaged, what to click), survives an app restart, refuses the retry while the drive is away with the same remedy, and finishes in two clicks when it is back. A capture beside an export does not hold the export up, a message re-captured during the export is told apart from one damaged on disk and from one removed, and a retry exports the new copy. The Settings-save guard asks before a save retargets the weekly backup, names the account and the folder, writes nothing until answered, and its three choices do what they say. A helper with no app can verify an archive-sourced export with the README's own commands (all files OK), and an inherited archive folder explains itself without any software (its README says to open `index.html`).

What failed was the unplanned road. A stopped export is welded to the archive's path (F1). The gate that refuses links and hard links (a control, F22) is met only per message after Start, with no word about what to do (F2). A button the card offers is refused when clicked (F3). The words that tell the owner to run "Rebuild search index" are wrong for another folder and hide that Rebuild forgets records whose files are missing (F4). And an archive that went away is described as one that never existed (F5).

## Findings

Blockers first (F1-F4), then the rest, most valuable first. "In scope" is this branch's. Each finding says what the walk saw and a suggested remedy; nothing was fixed.

**F1 · [Type II · blocker · in scope · OW-9] A stopped archive export cannot be finished once its archive has moved.**
The retry re-resolves against the archive "as it is now" (rulings §13.18), but it is bound to the archive's path, by design (RR2-C4). After R-o2 the natural response to a failing drive is to copy the archive to another one. Then the card still offers Export the remaining messages and every click is refused: for the configured archive after Settings is repointed, R-l2 "This selection was made in the archive at <old>, but Settings now points at <new> — Load folders again to start a new selection", whose remedy re-exports all 40 messages, not the 35 that remain, and nothing says the 5 already exported are in the first export; for another folder, R-o2 "isn't available (it was disconnected or moved) — Reconnect it", for a folder that cannot be reconnected. Transcripts `p-retry-after-the-archive-moved-*`. *Suggested:* a Locate… for the archive on the card (as there is for an export folder), re-binding when every key the retry needs is in the manifest found there with the fixity the plan recorded, or at least R-l2 and R-o2 words for a retry that name the real options (set Archive location back, or select again, which exports everything).

**F2 · [Type III · blocker · in scope · OW-10] Links in an archive are found per message after Start, with no remedy.**
A hard-link backup's copy (rsnapshot, `cp -al`, a deduplicating tool) or a store folder moved to another disk and linked back previews clean. For the hard-linked copy the Preview is correct and silent (3 of 6 have an original; the message list is "unavailable" with F4's advice). For the linked store the Preview calls a file it cannot examine "there" (an Lstat error other than "not there" counts as present, `ArchiveSource.exists`), so it says "Of 6 selected, 6 have an original .eml" when 3 have none, and "about 414 B (3 of unknown size)" is the only sign. Start then exports 0 of 6 (hard links) or 3 of 6 (links), with reasons "the archive's file has more than one hard link, so MailArchive can't be sure it is the archive's own" and "a link stands in the archive where this file should be, so it was not followed". The gate is a control (F22); the missing sentence is not: copy the archive to a plain folder with a copy that does not keep links, then choose that. The failure rows also carry no subject when the list is unavailable (" — alice — …"). *Suggested:* the Preview runs the same gate on what it counts (it already Lstats each record's files) and says, before Start, how many files it will refuse and why and what to do; the reasons carry the remedy.

**F3 · [Type III · blocker · in scope · OW-10, OW-8] A button is offered and refused: "Export failed messages again" when every failure is terminal.**
`endedActions` offers it for any partial run with a failed count above zero; the archive adds terminal reasons (links, removed, damaged), so a run whose failures are all terminal offers a retry whose click is refused R-h "There are no messages left to export from this export." (no what, why or remedy), and the card keeps the button. The design's own table says "no retry is offered" for the 256 MiB case, which is a terminal failure of the same kind. *Suggested:* offer it only when the run has a retryable failure not covered by another part (the walk the retry already makes), and word R-h as "The N messages that failed can't be tried again: <reason>".

**F4 · [Type III · blocker · in scope · INH-4, OW-14, OW-16] "Rebuild search index" is named where it cannot help, and where it forgets.**
(a) Another archive folder is read-only and Maintenance's Rebuild acts on the configured archive, but the list's notes ("This archive has no search index … Rebuild search index (Maintenance) brings the list back", "… can't be used for a message list … fixes the list", "N messages in this folder are not in the search index — Rebuild search index fixes the list") and the preview's warning (the same sentence nested in "The archive's search index can't be read (…), so most messages …") are the same words for both. For the inheritor there is no action in the sentence that can be taken. (b) "N records whose files were removed are skipped — run Rebuild search index (Maintenance) to bring the list and the counts back in step": Rebuild prunes every record whose file is missing (`internal/app/reindex.go`: `manifest.Delete(key)`, "N pruned"). That is right for a redaction and wrong for a file lost to a disk fault, where the record is the only proof the message existed and Check integrity is the step that says. The sentence names no consequence and does not tell the two apart. (c) The list gives the same advice for an unplugged drive (F5). *Suggested:* for another folder say what is true ("nothing in MailArchive can repair this folder; copy it to a folder of your own and make that the archive location, or export whole folders"); for skipped records say "removed on purpose? Rebuild forgets them. Missing by accident? Run Check integrity first".

**F5 · [Type III · in scope · OW-6] An archive that went away is described as one that never existed.**
After the archive disappears between two clicks the page says: the list, "no search index"; Load folders and Start, R-o "There's no archive at X yet — run Archive now first" (on a Mac or Linux where the mount point folder stays, Archive now would create a new archive in it); the Source line, the same; for another folder, R-l2 "choose the folder again", then R-o3 "isn't a MailArchive archive (it has no manifest)". The page loaded this archive moments ago and knows it. *Suggested:* when a session held the archive and it is gone, one sentence for every route: "The archive at X isn't there any more (disconnected or moved?). Reconnect it, then click …".

**F6 · [Type II · in scope · OW-5] Failures recorded before the archive is seen to be gone are terminal and never offered again.**
`classifyFiles` lists a missing file as terminal "removed from the archive after you selected it" unless the probe already says the archive is gone; messages that fail in the window before the folder itself disappears (a share failing file by file, a folder being deleted under the run) are listed that way, are not retryable, and after the archive is back the set says "1,152 of 1,200 are exported so far" with no button for the rest. Modelled with a deletion, not a unplug (an atomic loss is clean: 5 exported, R-o2, nothing listed). *Suggested:* when a run stops with R-o2, re-class the same run's earlier "removed" failures as retryable.

**F7 · [Type III · in scope · INH-1, OW-2, OW-14] The README and manifest of an archive-sourced export under-tell the recipient.**
(a) "Files whose names end .html, .attachments or .archived-page.txt … are conveniences made from the original, which stays the authoritative copy" is the Microsoft 365 export's sentence; for a message with no original the .html and the zip are the only copy. (b) The Counts line has no "without original" count (the manifest has `without_original`). (c) Gaps the archive recorded about a message (an attachment never downloaded, "completeness was not tracked by the version that captured this message", an unreadable part) are in that message's `notes` only: neither the preview nor the README says "N messages carry a note about what the archive itself lacks". (d) Nothing in the export says a message had left the mailbox when it was copied (the preview does). *Suggested:* one README sentence per kind that is present, with the count.

**F8 · [Type II · in scope · INH-1, OW-3] The README and manifest name every store the archive holds.**
Exporting one folder of bob's to hand to someone writes "It holds: alice, bob." (with mailbox addresses where the store has one: "alice (alice@contoso.org), bob") in the README and every store in `source.stores`, though only bob's folder was selected; the preview does not say so. For an owner with a multi-mailbox archive handing one user's folder to that user, the recipient learns the other mailboxes. Design XC21 says the manifest records the archive's mailboxes, so this is a choice. *Suggested:* name the stores of the selected messages; keep the full list out of what a third party receives, or say in the preview that it will be there.

**F9 · [Type III · in scope · OW-1, OW-3, INH-3] Export activity does not say which archive.**
The card's subtitle is "<when> · archive · <folder>" for the configured archive and for another folder; `mailbox` is empty. An owner with an archive of their own and an inherited one, or two exports a week apart, cannot tell the cards apart without opening them. *Suggested:* "another archive folder: <name>" or the configured archive's folder name, and the store labels.

**F10 · [Type III · in scope · OW-11, OW-3] Typed-path words for Another archive folder.**
A path that does not exist (a typo) or is a file reads "<path> isn't a MailArchive archive (it has no .mailarchive-manifest.json)": it says the folder exists. A path pasted with quotes, which is what Explorer's Copy as path gives, reads `"<path>" isn't a full path to an archive folder` while looking like a full path (the destination box does the same). The example in R-o3 and the box's placeholder is `/Users/you/Downloads`, a folder that never holds an archive. A manifest that is a folder reads "(it has no … that is a file: it is a link or a folder)". The dotfile's name is the first thing named, and the file is hidden in Finder and Explorer. *Suggested:* strip one pair of surrounding quotes; say "There is no folder at X" when it does not exist; lead with "the archive's top folder, the one that holds index.html and README.txt"; use an archive-shaped example.

**F11 · [Type III · in scope · OW-11] Unreadable, truncated and empty manifests.**
A manifest cut short by an interrupted copy is R-p, the id of "a newer MailArchive", with the engine's text: "manifest <path> is corrupt or truncated (unexpected end of JSON input): restore it from a backup, or delete it to re-export everything …". For an inherited read-only folder "delete it" is advice to modify what the app promises never to touch, and "restore from a backup" assumes one. A manifest of zero bytes (a sync that has not finished) loads as an archive of 0 records: an empty tree and no sentence ("holds:" is left out and the footer says 0 messages). *Suggested:* "This copy of the archive is incomplete (its manifest is cut short) — copy the archive again from where you got it"; "This archive holds no messages yet (its manifest is empty)".

**F12 · [Type III · in scope · OW-4] A read failure is worded as a write failure.**
R-o2's why for an archive whose folder became unreadable reads "(the folder is write-protected, or this account isn't allowed to write to it: permission denied)": `leaveName` is the destination's wording. MailArchive only reads the archive. *Suggested:* "this account can't read it".

**F13 · [Type III · in scope · INH-5] The Settings-save guard.**
It works and it is the right ask. It does not say that reading an inherited archive no longer needs Settings (cancel and use Extraction workspace > Another archive folder); the primary-styled button (`btn`, the others are `btn secondary`) is Save and keep the backup, the harmful one; after Save and remove the note is "Settings saved, and the weekly backup removed." with no word on how to install it again, and putting the location back asks nothing and says nothing about the backup; clearing the box asks "Your weekly backup will now archive alice@contoso.org into ."; with nobody signed in it says "the Microsoft 365 account you are signed in as"; a failed removal shows "schtasks /Delete failed: Access is denied. (0x80070005)". *Suggested:* a sentence in the dialog about the read-only way, Cancel as the primary, the Weekly backup card named after a removal.

**F14 · [Type II · in scope · OW-16, INH-4] An archive without a usable index exports anonymously.**
Without `search.db` (a copy that skipped it) or with a damaged one, every message in the export is `0000-00-00_0000_untitled_<id>.eml` under `0000-00/`, with an empty subject, sender and date in the manifest, although the `.eml` (or the archived page) is in hand and has them. The preview warns once, in a nested sentence, and Start is one click. *Suggested:* take the subject and date from the bytes it already reads, or ask once before Start.

**F15 · [Type II · in scope · OW-17] Thirty idle minutes lose the selection, in words that do not say why.**
The archive snapshot is released after 30 idle minutes (delegated, §13.18); the next click is R-j2 "A folder in this selection wasn't listed in this session — the page may be out of date", and Load folders clears the selection. Whole-folder entries are paths that resolve against a new snapshot; only a picked row's handle cannot. *Suggested:* re-open the snapshot when a request names folder ids the page listed, and say "you were away for more than 30 minutes" when it cannot.

**F16 · [Type III · in scope · OW-3, INH-3] The inheritor's first screen sends them to a tool they do not have.**
With no Microsoft 365 set up the page opens on the Microsoft 365 source with "isn't set up yet — … run mailarchive setup there (a separate download), then come back", Load folders disabled, and the way forward (the Source dropdown) is above it. *Suggested:* a line under the Source "To read an archive you were given, choose Another archive folder", or start on the archive source when Microsoft 365 is not set up.

**F17 · [Type III · in scope · OW-7, OW-8] The remedy words name a verb the app calls something else.**
"…(modified or damaged): run Verify on the archive", "(Verify records one)" and the manifest note "run Verify on the archive to record one" name the command-line verb; the Desktop button is "Check integrity" (Maintenance), and for another archive folder nothing in Desktop can run it. The two failures that stay after a retry (damaged, removed) give no next step. *Suggested:* the `rebuildButton`-style constant for the owner's words, and a sentence for the terminal ones ("restore the file from a backup of the archive, then export again").

**F18 · [Type III · in scope · OW-7] A preview during Rebuild warns without a remedy.**
"The search index is being rebuilt, so most messages in this export carry no subject, sender or date in their names and in the manifest." The remedy is to wait and preview again (the list's own note says so). *Suggested:* add it.

**F19 · [Type III, minor · in scope · OW-14, OW-8] Counts of one.**
"1 are no longer in the mailbox", "1 archived pages don't begin …", "1 originals have no checksum …", "1 records whose files were removed are skipped", "1 messages" and "Of 1 selected, 1 have an original .eml".

**F20 · [Type III, minor · in scope · OW-13] R-f says "your archive" for another folder.**
"That folder is inside your archive (<inherited folder>)": the path is right, the possessive is not. The design's text is the same; for the inheritor "the archive folder you chose" would be.

**F21 · [Type III · out of scope · INH-2] The command-line `verify` and `extract` on a read-only archive.**
`mailarchive verify -out <read-only folder>` and `extract` stop with "open lock …/.mailarchive.lock: permission denied" and no remedy, and `status` prints a WARN about a missing schedule to someone who only inherited the folder. Unchanged by this branch; the read-only workspace source is the app's answer. Backlog for the CLI's owner.

**F22 · [Type I · in scope · all archive cells] Counted ceremonies, not faulted.**
The archive gate refuses symlinks, irregular files, hard links and escapes (XC19), and a checksum mismatch (the message is listed, not copied); the Preview before every Start (X14); R-f and R-f2 refusing a destination that cannot be proven outside the archive (R-f2 could not be produced here: no share or FUSE mount); the read-only open that leaves a `-wal` unread and says so; Check integrity before trusting a damaged file; the Settings guard's three-way ask; and that "the remaining messages" are computed from the run's record against the archive as it is now. F1, F2 and F3 are about what the actor is told, not about removing these.

## What the walk could not cover

- **Page behaviour after a click (all JavaScript).** No browser, JS engine or screen reader: the Source control, Browse for another folder, `checkOther` on a typed path, Load folders, the tree and Load more, the retry's `workspace#preview=` hop, focus after Load folders disables itself, keyboard-only and screen-reader reading. All of it is read from the served script; the owner's click-through of the three scripts (E3's OW-19) is still owed and now covers the Source control too.
- **A real unplug.** The folder losing its contents (a deletion after Start) and becoming unreadable (permission bits 000 after Start, and at the exact message with the step hook) were used; a pulled USB stick (EIO) and a dropped SMB share were not. F5 and F6 depend on how the OS reports the first failed read.
- **R-f2** (a share on this computer, FUSE, overlay, a UNC archive through another view) and **Windows** altogether: fileLinks, reparse points, `subst`, share-delete snapshots (XC20), Explorer's Copy as path (walked as a quoted path on macOS). Words read from `copyexport_dest.go` and the MA-512 tests; the Windows legs are vet-checked only (§13.18).
- **A capture running at the same instant.** The archive's lock was held by a test (`lockfile.AcquireAs`) and a real capture ran between two steps; a capture writing while an export reads was not raced. The Rebuild job was modelled by the workspace's own `beginRebuild` flag, not run.
- **Read-only media** was modelled with permission bits for a user who is not root. A DVD that also stores no `search.db-shm` was not made.
- **A list that lacks rows** (the "N messages … are not in the search index" note): the deletion in the copy's index matched no rows, so the note was read, not seen. An over-200,000 folder was walked with the cap lowered to 100.
- **Throughput.** On this loaded machine 1,200 messages took about 15 s (about 80 a second); a large archive's export time was not measured.
- **The desktop binary** was built at the head and its `-h` read (`-no-workspace` among its flags); its pages sit behind the one-time link, so they were reached through the same router in-process. The command-line binary was built and run for the inheritor's read-only walk (INH-2).
- **Not part of this review:** the E4 adversarial addendum (archive read gates, the immutable open, the archived-page gate, concurrency, Windows share mode), which §13.18 also owes; the unfiltered suite.
